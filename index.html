Blended intelligence, not just numbers

Skating data alone can't tell the whole story. ICA's composite score weights observational evaluation at 60% — capturing compete level, hockey sense, skills, and character — then blends in Drive Hockey's objective skating metrics at 40%.

All Drive Hockey metrics are normalized on a percentile scale of 25–100 within each age group, so your athlete's score means something in the context of their peers — not just an abstract number.

  • Consistent evaluators ensure comparability across events
  • Percentile normalization gives age-appropriate context
  • Repeated events build a true development trajectory
  • Separate goalie methodology for position-specific accuracy

Five skating metrics — normalized by age group

ICA uses five key Drive Hockey metrics in our composite score, each normalized on a percentile scale of 25–100 within your age group so every score is meaningful in context.

Top Speed
Maximum velocity captured during full-flight skating events. How fast can your athlete actually skate?
Acceleration
Explosive first-step burst — distance gained in the first 1.5 and 3.0 seconds from a stationary start, moving start, and with puck.
Agility
Edge quality, turn speed, and transition skating measured through angular skating events during play.
Conditioning
Force capacity — how well a player maintains peak output over time. The percentage of maximum force they sustain across a session.
Deceleration
Edge braking and stopping power — critical for positioning, puck battles, and defensive zone play.
